🚨 SURVIVAL GUIDE: How to Prevent Accidents and Prepare for an Earthquake

LIMA. Due to Peru's geographical location on the Pacific Ring of Fire [El Peruano], the Geophysical Institute of Peru (IGP) and INDECI emphasize that while earthquakes cannot be predicted, tragic consequences can be avoided through active prevention and family preparation [El Peruano, Infobae].

📌 Key Actions to Prevent Accidents

  1. Identify Safe Zones: Locate columns, load-bearing walls, and strong structural areas inside your home, school, or workplace.
  2. Clear Evacuation Routes: Keep corridors, doors, and stairs completely free of furniture, heavy pots, or toys that could block your path.
  3. Secure Heavy Objects: Firmly anchor wardrobes, bookshelves, shelves, and television sets to the wall to prevent them from falling during shaking.
  4. Organize a Family Drill: Assign specific roles (who opens the door, who assists pets or elderly relatives) and define a safe outdoor meeting point.

🎒 How to Build an Emergency Go-Bag

The emergency backpack should be placed in an easily accessible location near the exit door, and its contents should be calculated to sustain two people during the first 24 hours [Infobae].

Indispensable Items Your Backpack Must Contain:

  • Hygiene Supplies: Antibacterial gel, toilet paper, hand towels, and wet wipes.
  • First Aid Kit: Antiseptic alcohol, sterile gauze, bandages, medical tape, and chronic prescription medications for family members.
  • Food and Water: Still bottled water and non-perishable canned food (tuna, beans) with a manual can opener.
  • Warmth and Tools: A fleece blanket, flashlight, portable battery-powered radio (to listen to emergency updates), and a whistle.
  • Communication and ID: Duplicate house keys, cash (coins), and plastic-sealed copies of national identification cards (DNI).
